Atlas Data Views · d3

Nine ways d3 could add to the Tel Aviv Atlas, drawn from the app's real data: buildings in data/buildings.ts. Tap any dot to open that building in the running app.

Style emergence

Every building on one time axis, coloured by style. Filled dots are firm dates; hollow rings are approximate (“Early 1920s”, “c. 1932”).

Improves: the drag-only StyleTimeline. You see the Eclectic → International Style hand-over around 1930 at a glance, and the hollow rings show which dates still need archive work.

Architects' careers

One row per architect, a dot per building, a line from first to last. Sorted by when each architect first appears.

Improves: the architects index. It shows who overlapped with whom (Berlin and Magidovitch, say) and where each career turned from Eclectic to Modern. The rows link straight to the bio pages.

The city grows

Buildings placed by their geocoded coordinates. Drag the year, or press play, to watch the city fill in.

1960

Improves: the map screen. A time filter turns the map from a static list of pins into the story of how the secular city grew outward from Jaffa and Neve Tzedek.

Research gaps

How many buildings have a named architect and a firm year — the two headline facts — and which ones are still open.

Improves: your own research loop. It's the “architect + exact year” priority as a live checklist, and each open item is one tap from its building page.

Building waves

How many buildings in the atlas were built in each 5-year span, stacked by style. Firm and approximate dates both count, placed by their first year.

Improves: the Style pages. Each one could open with its own wave, showing when the style peaked and how it overlapped with the others.

Who built what style

Architects (rows) against styles (columns). The darker the cell, the more buildings. Tap a cell to list them.

Improves: the architect bio pages. It answers whether someone switched styles or stayed in one lane, from the atlas's own entries.

Style → architect → building

A treemap: each block is one building, grouped by architect inside its style. Tap to open the building.

Improves: the Atlas index. A single screen shows the whole collection's shape, including how much of it is Berlin and Eclectic.

Distance from Jaffa

Each building's distance from the Jaffa Clock Tower against its year, with a trend line.

Improves: the secular-city story. It tests in numbers whether the city grew northward away from Jaffa, and the outliers (buildings far off the line) are worth a story card.

Decade by decade

The same map split into small panels, one per decade. Dots from earlier decades stay grey for context.

Improves: walking tours. Each panel is one era's walk, a ready-made route for “Tel Aviv in the 1930s”.
